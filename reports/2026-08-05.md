# August 5, 2026 — Tech & Markets Morning Debrief

- **Bending Spoons agreed to buy Airtable for $1.285 billion in cash** (about $2.25 billion including Airtable's net cash), marking the Italian software roll-up's first acquisition since its own July IPO. The price is roughly an 80% haircut to Airtable's $11.7 billion valuation from 2021, a sharp signal that the SaaS down-round reckoning is now spilling into outright acquisitions.
- **Palantir crushed Q2 estimates**, posting EPS of $0.41 versus $0.35 expected and revenue of $1.94 billion, beating estimates by more than 7%. Shares surged roughly 29.5%, reinforcing the market's appetite for AI-software names with real revenue growth to back up their valuations.
- **SpaceX's first earnings report as a public company disappointed investors despite a headline beat.** Q2 revenue hit $7.8 billion (vs. ~$6.8 billion expected), but AI-related capex jumped to $15.8 billion from $7.7 billion in Q1, and shares fell more than 8%. Musk also committed SpaceX's AI data centers to Nvidia GPUs exclusively going forward.
- **AMD posted $11.5 billion in Q2 revenue, edging past estimates**, with Data Center segment sales up 107% year-over-year to $6.7 billion — nearly 60% of total revenue. The beat wasn't enough to fully impress investors, who are increasingly focused on the pace of AI capex spend across the sector.
- **Apple escalated its legal fight with OpenAI**, filing for a preliminary injunction to block or force a redesign of OpenAI's in-development AI hardware (including a rumored screenless "AI phone"), alleging former Apple engineers funneled iPhone and product-design trade secrets to OpenAI's hardware unit. A win for Apple could meaningfully delay OpenAI's push into consumer devices.
- **Electronic Arts' $55 billion take-private buyout officially closed**, with Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund taking roughly 93% ownership alongside Affinity Partners and Silver Lake — one of the largest tech/gaming buyouts on record now finalized and off the public market.
- **The Dow and Nasdaq both closed at fresh record highs**, up 1.7% and 2.6% respectively, powered by the wave of strong AI-linked earnings (Palantir, AMD) and easing crude oil prices.

Earnings season is doing more to move the market right now than new dealmaking — AI infrastructure spend is simultaneously the bull case (Palantir's demand story, AMD's Data Center growth) and the emerging worry (SpaceX's capex spike hitting margins and sentiment despite a revenue beat). Watch **PLTR**, **AMD**, and **SPCX** for continued swings around AI capex commentary, along with **AAPL** as its OpenAI injunction fight plays out — a favorable ruling would be a clear near-term positive for Apple's competitive position in AI hardware. The Airtable fire-sale is also worth watching as a bellwether: if more richly-valued 2021-era SaaS unicorns start trading hands at steep discounts, expect more consolidation deals like Bending Spoons' to follow.
