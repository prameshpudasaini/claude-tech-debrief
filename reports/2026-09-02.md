# 2026-09-02

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Broadcom reports fiscal Q3 earnings today after the close** — the quarter's biggest AI-chip catalyst. Wall Street expects ~$16B in AI semiconductor revenue (up over 200% year-over-year) against CEO Hock Tan's disclosed $30B+ AI bookings backlog. Broadcom's custom accelerator business counts Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta as customers, making this print a bellwether for the whole AI-infrastructure trade after last quarter's guidance-driven selloff.
- **Dell Technologies jumped ~8% in premarket trading** after beating on both revenue and earnings and raising its fiscal 2027 guidance, citing strength in AI server sales — a read-through that hyperscaler and enterprise AI infrastructure spending is still accelerating.
- **AI coding startup Cognition (maker of Devin) is closing a roughly $1 billion round that would value it at about $47 billion**, per Bloomberg, after pulling in nearly $10 billion in investor demand — one of the largest AI startup valuations minted this week and a marker of how hot the agentic-coding subsector remains.
- **Nvidia backed a $125 million Series B for iPronics**, a Spanish silicon-photonics startup building programmable optical circuit switches for AI data centers. The round (co-led by Maverick Silicon and Light Street Capital) is Nvidia's latest strategic bet on the networking/optics layer underpinning AI infrastructure, alongside Bosch Ventures and others.
- **Optical-photonics firm Starman Optical is acquiring a 90% stake in GoPro for $285 million ($1.14/share)**, with GoPro remaining Nasdaq-listed. The deal pivots the struggling camera maker toward AI data-center optical transceivers, defense, and aerospace — another example of legacy hardware names repositioning around AI infrastructure demand.
- **Marvell's Google custom-chip tie-up kept generating headlines this week**: CEO Matt Murphy said the warrant-linked deal, tied to up to $120 billion in cumulative revenue through 2033, could end up "a lot larger" than Wall Street currently models — a continuing overhang/catalyst for MRVL shares heading into its next print.

## What It Means

The through-line remains AI infrastructure capex, but the story is broadening beyond the big labs and GPU makers into the supply chain around them — custom silicon (Broadcom, Marvell), servers (Dell), and now optical networking (iPronics, and GoPro's forced pivot into data-center optics). Broadcom's after-hours print is the single most important catalyst of the day: a beat-and-raise on AI bookings would extend the "AI capex is still under-modeled" narrative that lifted Dell and Marvell this week, while any hint of demand softening would ripple across AVGO, MRVL, and the broader semiconductor complex. Meanwhile, fresh mega-rounds like Cognition's $47B valuation show private AI investors are still paying up aggressively even as public investors get pickier about which AI infrastructure bets pay off — worth watching AVGO overnight, DELL and MRVL into the open, and GPRO for how the market prices its pivot.
