# 2026-09-17

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Amazon signed an up-to-$8 billion, seven-year supply deal with Generac** for backup power generators at its AI data centers, taking warrants for roughly 1.7 million Generac shares (worth up to ~$340M); GNRC shares soared as much as 45% after-hours and stayed sharply higher Thursday, with initial deliveries of $2.4B slated for 2027–2028 — another sign AI power-infrastructure suppliers are becoming warrant-linked "strategic partners" for hyperscalers, following Oracle's Bloom Energy stake earlier this year.
- **Intel and SK Hynix talks over a first-ever U.S. memory-chip manufacturing tie-up gained momentum**, with Korean traders reportedly rotating out of Samsung and into SK Hynix on the news; Intel shares extended gains (+3–5% this week) and SK Hynix's ADR rose further, even though SK Hynix reiterated that "no decisions have been made" on either the Ohio-fab-lease or joint-venture scenarios.
- **GlobalFoundries and Marvell Technology expanded their chip-capacity agreement** to boost production of a specialized semiconductor used in high-speed optical connections for AI data centers, addressing surging demand for data-center interconnect capacity — a read-through positive for both GFS and MRVL as AI networking spend keeps climbing.
- **OpenAI acquired Glass Imaging**, a startup founded by former Apple engineers that uses AI to improve smartphone-camera image processing, in a deal valuing the company at more than $300 million — OpenAI's latest bolt-on as it pushes further into consumer hardware/imaging, a space that overlaps directly with Apple's core camera differentiation.
- **Huawei announced plans to launch a new AI chip in Q1 2027** aimed squarely at competing with Nvidia, underscoring China's continued push for chip self-sufficiency despite U.S. export restrictions — a longer-term competitive overhang worth tracking for Nvidia's China-exposure narrative even though near-term revenue impact is limited.
- **The Fed delivered a 25-basis-point rate hike Wednesday** (its first in three years), and tech stocks rebounded hard Thursday as Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish-but-credible tone reassured investors on inflation; the Nasdaq jumped about 1.4% in early trading, outpacing the Dow (+0.7%) and S&P 500 (+1%), with chip and AI-infrastructure names among the biggest gainers.

## What It Means

Today's clearest theme is AI infrastructure spending spilling into unglamorous but essential supply chains — power generation (Amazon/Generac), memory (Intel/SK Hynix), and data-center interconnects (GlobalFoundries/Marvell) — with hyperscalers increasingly using warrants to lock in capacity from smaller suppliers, a structure worth watching for read-throughs to other power and components names. Watch GNRC for follow-through after its 45% pop, INTC/SKHY for any formal Ohio-fab announcement, and MRVL/GFS on the networking-capacity story. Longer term, Huawei's Nvidia-rival chip timeline and OpenAI's continued bolt-on acquisitions (Glass Imaging following its imaging/hardware ambitions) are worth filing away as competitive signals even though their market impact is more gradual than today's supply-chain deals; combined with a market-friendly Fed decision, the setup favors continued near-term strength in AI-infrastructure-adjacent stocks.
