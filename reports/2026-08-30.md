# 2026-08-30

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Nvidia paused its AI-cloud revenue-sharing financing program over antitrust concerns.** The "AI Compute Partnership," launched in July to help smaller cloud providers finance GPU purchases in exchange for Nvidia taking a cut of rental revenue, was pulled back less than two months in after internal staff and prospective partners flagged control and antitrust risk. Nvidia says its broader financing push is "still in place," but the retreat is a read on how much scrutiny AI infrastructure deals are now drawing. Watch NVDA and the neocloud names (CoreWeave, Lambda, Crusoe) that were counting on the program.
- **OpenAI is cutting off Cursor's access to its models after SpaceX completed its $60B acquisition of Cursor-maker Anysphere.** OpenAI gave the maximum contractual notice — November 12 — citing distrust of how Musk-controlled companies honor licensing terms, pointing to Twitter/X's earlier break with OpenAI. Cursor still has Anthropic, Google and SpaceXAI models in-editor, but the spat is another flashpoint in the Musk-Altman rivalry with real product implications for AI coding tools.
- **Quantum computing firm Pasqal went public via SPAC merger with Bleichroeder Acquisition Corp. II, debuting on Nasdaq (PSQL).** Shares surged 40–52% on their first trading day, valuing the French neutral-atom quantum firm at roughly $2B and handing it $360M in cash to fund its push toward fault-tolerant quantum processors — a strong signal of investor appetite for quantum names re-entering public markets.
- **Nvidia-backed neocloud Lambda raised about $1B in private short-term debt to buy more GPUs, tied to chips it's leasing to Microsoft.** JPMorgan arranged the placement, the latest in a string of debt raises (including a $926M loan earlier this month) as Lambda builds out infrastructure ahead of a reported $3B pre-IPO round — another data point in the mountain of AI-related debt (over $400B raised globally in 2026 so far).
- **Marvell lifted its FY2027 revenue guidance to roughly $12B and FY2028 to $18B, citing 46% growth in its data-center chip business.** The raise comes as Marvell rides its expanded custom-silicon partnership with Google, reinforcing that hyperscaler AI-chip diversification (away from pure Nvidia/Broadcom dependence) is translating into real, guided revenue growth.
- **AWS and Nvidia expanded their infrastructure pact to deploy 2 million additional Blackwell Ultra, Rubin and Rubin Ultra GPUs across AWS's global footprint through 2027–2028.** The buildout targets agentic AI, scientific computing and physical/robotics workloads; Amazon shares actually traded lower on the news as investors weighed the capex load, even as it locks in Nvidia's next two years of hyperscaler demand.

## What it means

The dominant theme is AI infrastructure spending starting to collide with real-world friction: antitrust concerns are forcing Nvidia to walk back parts of its cloud-financing playbook, and a $2M-GPU capex commitment from AWS drew a *negative* stock reaction as investors start questioning how much more balance-sheet risk hyperscalers can absorb. At the same time, capital keeps flowing to prove out the theme — Lambda's latest $1B debt raise, Marvell's guidance beat off its Google chip deal, and Pasqal's strong SPAC debut all show investors are still rewarding AI/quantum infrastructure plays, just with more discrimination than a few months ago. The OpenAI-Cursor-SpaceX rift is a reminder that model-access dependencies are a real business risk for AI-native software companies, not just a Musk-Altman soap opera. Near-term, watch NVDA for how the financing-program pullback plays with regulators, MRVL for follow-through on its guidance raise, AMZN for capex-related pressure, and PSQL as a bellwether for whether the quantum-computing public-market rally has legs.
