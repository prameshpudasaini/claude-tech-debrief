# 2026-08-21

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Nvidia is paying $10B to effectively absorb Poolside's model-building tech.** The deal splits into a $6B non-exclusive license for Poolside's AI model software plus a $1B equity investment at a $12B pre-money valuation. Nvidia will extend job offers to 109 Poolside engineers behind its "Laguna" model project and gain access to its "Model Factory" production system — a talent-and-IP acquihire in disguise that keeps Nvidia's foundation-model ambitions moving without a full buyout.
- **Broadcom is lining up $60–100B in debt to bankroll AI chip production for Anthropic and others.** Bloomberg reports Broadcom is negotiating with lenders — reportedly including Apollo and Blackstone — on a structure with a roughly $30B junior tranche plus a larger senior-secured piece, echoing the debt-funded chip-supply playbook already used elsewhere in AI infrastructure. This follows Broadcom's separate multiyear pact with OpenAI to build custom chips and networking gear for 10 gigawatts of AI data-center capacity.
- **Anthropic's IPO is coming into sharper focus.** Reports point to an October 2026 Nasdaq listing that could value the company near $2 trillion — which would eclipse SpaceX's record June debut — with Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley leading. Anthropic's annualized revenue reportedly hit roughly $65B by end of July, up sharply from year-end 2025.
- **Alibaba is selling its gaming arm to fund its AI pivot.** Trustar Capital is acquiring Lingxi Games (maker of "Three Kingdoms: Strategy Edition") for at least $1.5B, with some reports citing over $2B to Alibaba — part of CEO Eddie Wu's push to shed non-core assets and concentrate capital on AI and cloud.
- **Two more tech-adjacent names priced IPOs this week.** Space-structures startup Gravitics Holdings set terms for a $125M Nasdaq listing (ticker GVTX, $14–$17/share) via reverse merger, while defense-tech roll-up Lyntris priced a downsized IPO at $17.50, below its marketed range — a sign investor appetite for smaller tech IPOs is selective even as mega-cap AI listings draw huge demand.
- **Nasdaq is expanding into off-exchange trading infrastructure.** Its definitive agreement to acquire LeveL Markets — the third-largest U.S. alternative trading system by volume — folds into Nasdaq's new Digital Liquidity Networks unit, terms undisclosed.

## What it means

The through-line today is AI infrastructure financing shifting from equity to debt: Broadcom's $60–100B lender talks and the broader trend of chipmakers borrowing heavily to fund capacity mirror how capital-intensive the AI buildout has become, and it's a read-through for AVGO, NVDA and credit markets broadly. Nvidia's Poolside deal shows the giants are still willing to pay up for model-building talent and IP even as they lean on debt elsewhere. Watch NVDA and AVGO for follow-through on chip-supply news, ANTHROPIC-adjacent names (and Nasdaq itself, given its listing ambitions) as the October IPO timeline firms up, and BABA on execution of its AI-focused divestitures.
