# 2026-09-14

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei publicly called for the AI industry to slow the pace of frontier model development**, warning of mounting safety risks (loss of control, bioterrorism, cyberattack misuse); OpenAI's Sam Altman and xAI's Elon Musk both backed the call within hours, an unusual show of unity among rival AI labs that triggered a broad selloff in AI infrastructure and chip stocks in Monday trading.
- **OpenAI delayed its planned IPO out of 2026 and into 2027**, with Sam Altman telling Fortune that going public now would be "ill-advised" given the safety concerns swirling around the industry — removing what would have been one of the year's largest tech listings from near-term market calendars.
- **Nvidia is in talks to invest up to $10 billion in Anthropic's IPO** as an anchor investor, part of a listing that could raise as much as $100 billion and value Anthropic near $2 trillion, reportedly targeted to price before the November U.S. midterms; the potential stake deepens Nvidia's financial ties to one of its largest AI customers.
- **President Trump publicly rejected the AI slowdown push**, telling reporters "whoever wins AI wins" and citing competition with China — a pointed signal that Washington is unlikely to back voluntary industry pauses, adding policy uncertainty to the market reaction alongside the CEOs' safety warnings.
- **Qualcomm's $60 billion custom AI chip partnership with Amazon continues to be a focal point for chip-sector investors**, with the multi-generational inference-silicon and optical-interconnect deal (first disclosed September 8) still shaping sentiment on which suppliers benefit as hyperscalers diversify away from Nvidia.
- **Wearables maker Oura is pressing ahead with a Nasdaq IPO (ticker OURA)** that could raise up to $3 billion and value the company above $16 billion, positioning it as one of the next tech listings to watch even as the broader IPO pipeline (including OpenAI's) shows signs of caution.

## What It Means

Today's dominant story is a sentiment shock, not a deal: a rare joint call from Anthropic, OpenAI, and xAI's chiefs to slow frontier AI development spooked investors who have spent two years pricing in ever-accelerating AI capex, sending chipmakers and data-center suppliers sharply lower in Monday trading. The message is being read two ways — as a genuine safety pivot, and as a signal that even AI leaders see the current spending pace as unsustainable — while Trump's immediate pushback on China competition suggests policy won't force a slowdown regardless of what labs say. Watch NVDA, AMD, and semiconductor-index names for whether today's drop extends or reverses, QCOM for continued strength on its Amazon silicon deal, and the IPO calendar (OpenAI now pushed to 2027, Anthropic's Nvidia-backed listing still on track for before the midterms, Oura pressing ahead) as the clearest read on whether capital markets share the industry's own new caution.
