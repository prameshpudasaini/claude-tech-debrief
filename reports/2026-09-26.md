# 2026-09-26

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Anthropic's IPO timeline has slipped again, from a targeted mid-October window to November**, per WSJ reporting — the AI startup wants to show Q3 financials (potentially its first GAAP profit) after OpenAI's Astra model launch, and is still seeking $100 billion at a $2 trillion valuation, which would make it the largest US IPO ever.
- **Databricks acquired spreadsheet startup Row Zero** to bring live, governed spreadsheets into its "Genie" AI coworker, its latest in a string of tuck-in acquisitions as it races Snowflake and Microsoft for enterprise AI-data workflows ahead of its own expected IPO.
- **SK Hynix stock jumped roughly 3% after a Reuters report that it's in talks with Intel to manufacture memory chips in the US**, potentially at Intel's planned Ohio fab — either by leasing capacity or forming a joint venture with cloud companies seeking secured memory supply; SK Hynix says no deal is finalized yet.
- **Oura set IPO pricing at $40–$44 per share**, targeting a fully diluted valuation of about $15.6 billion ahead of its Nasdaq debut (ticker OURA) during the week of September 28 — one of the largest consumer-hardware listings of the year and a read-through for wearables names like Garmin.
- **Priority Technology Holdings (PRTH) agreed to go private in a $1.6 billion, CEO-led buyout** at $8.05/share (a 65% premium), backed by Searchlight Capital Partners — a reminder that payments/fintech-tech consolidation continues alongside the AI-driven megadeals dominating headlines.

## What It Means

The big swing factor for near-term AI-sector sentiment is Anthropic's second IPO delay in as many months — pushing to November signals the company wants a clean profitability story before facing public markets, and every slip adds to prediction-market doubt about the record $2 trillion valuation actually landing this year. Elsewhere, the memory/chip supply chain keeps reshuffling: SK Hynix-Intel talks (watch INTC and MU for read-through) show how AI-driven memory scarcity is pulling manufacturing back into the US, while Databricks' Row Zero buy is another sign the AI-infrastructure arms race is now being fought over data workflows, not just chips. Oura's ~$15.6B IPO next week is the one to watch for the broader "will investors still pay up for growth" test heading into Q4, and Priority Technology's take-private shows fintech-adjacent tech consolidation hasn't slowed even as attention stays fixed on AI megadeals.
