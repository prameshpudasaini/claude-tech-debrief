# 2026-08-07

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals (last 24 hours)

- **Unitree prices Shanghai IPO at ~$9B valuation.** The Chinese humanoid-robot maker priced shares at 150.8 yuan ($22.34), valuing the company at roughly $9.04 billion, with AI lab DeepSeek putting in $20.8 million through the strategic placement — a fresh signal of capital flowing into the humanoid-robotics race.
- **Defense-tech startup Hadrian raises $1.37B at a $7.87B valuation.** A JPMorgan Strategic Investment Group-anchored Series D — with Morgan Stanley Wealth Management, Apollo, T. Rowe Price, a16z and Founders Fund also participating — more than quadruples Hadrian's valuation since January as it scales AI-run factories supplying parts to the Pentagon and Lockheed Martin.
- **Robinhood moves to give retail investors a piece of Y Combinator startups.** The brokerage is building a publicly accessible investment vehicle tied to YC-connected private companies, extending its push to bring pre-IPO exposure to everyday traders.
- **IonQ closes its $1.8B acquisition of SkyWater Technology.** With regulatory approval secured and the deal completed, IonQ becomes what it calls the only vertically integrated, full-stack quantum computing company with in-house chip fabrication; the combined entity held its first joint earnings call this week.
- **Samsung and SK hynix lock in roughly $950B in AI chip and infrastructure agreements.** The Korean memory giants converted existing supply arrangements with Broadcom, Nvidia and Microsoft into five-year long-term contracts, cementing their grip on the AI hardware supply chain — Samsung's Broadcom pact alone is worth $200 billion through 2030.
- **Meta and Microsoft reaffirm aggressive AI capex plans, lifting chip stocks.** Big Tech's ~$650 billion 2026 AI spending pace (Amazon ~$200B, Alphabet $175–185B capex) got fresh confirmation this week, sending Nvidia up 2.5% and pulling AMD along as investors bet the GPU buildout isn't slowing.

## What it means

The through-line today is capital chasing AI infrastructure and the robots/defense-manufacturing crossover — money is moving from cloud giants' balance sheets (Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet) straight into chip supply chains (Nvidia, AMD, Samsung, SK hynix, Broadcom), while private markets reward anything that can execute AI at scale, from humanoid robots (Unitree) to automated defense factories (Hadrian). Watch NVDA, AMD, and the Korean memory names for continued upside on the reaffirmed capex story, IONQ for reaction to the SkyWater integration, and HOOD if the YC investment vehicle gains regulatory traction. Collectively, these moves signal the AI investment cycle is broadening beyond pure-play chipmakers into adjacent hardware, defense, and retail-access products — a bullish near-term setup for the AI supply chain, but one that raises the bar for upcoming earnings to justify the spending.
