# August 4, 2026 — Tech & Markets Morning Debrief

- **Nvidia and SK Group expanded their AI infrastructure partnership, now valued at more than $500 billion**, spanning new AI-factory buildouts and next-generation HBM4 memory supply from SK hynix for Nvidia's upcoming Vera Rubin platform. The deal deepens Nvidia's grip on the AI memory supply chain and gives SK hynix a locked-in customer for years of high-margin output.
- **Intel shares jumped on fresh reports that Broadcom and TSMC are separately exploring bids to split the struggling chipmaker**, with Broadcom eyeing the chip-design and marketing business and TSMC weighing a takeover of Intel's fabs. Talks are still informal, and a White House official signaled resistance to a foreign firm operating Intel's U.S. factories — a political wrinkle that could complicate any deal.
- **Electronic Arts' $55 billion take-private buyout is set to close today**, handing roughly 93% ownership to Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund alongside Affinity Partners and Silver Lake. It's one of the largest tech/gaming buyouts on record and pulls a major publicly traded gaming name off the US market.
- **Alibaba unveiled its Qwen3.8-Max AI model**, sending its US-listed shares up about 4% as investors bet the release strengthens its position against OpenAI, Google, and domestic Chinese rivals in the large-model race.
- **ArcelorMittal deepened its technology partnership with Microsoft**, making Azure the backbone for modernizing its plant systems and adding other Microsoft infrastructure services — a reminder that Azure's growth story now extends well beyond pure software and cloud-native customers into heavy industry.
- **Prysmian agreed to acquire electrical-infrastructure maker Atkore for $3.8 billion in cash** ($95/share), sending Atkore shares up nearly 28%. The deal combines cable and conduit manufacturing to better serve data-center and grid buildout demand, underscoring how AI-driven infrastructure spending is rippling into adjacent industrial sectors.

Today's moves keep tracing the same fault line as recent weeks: AI infrastructure spend (Nvidia-SK's $500B memory and data-center pact, Prysmian-Atkore's data-center-adjacent buyout) keeps expanding even as legacy chipmakers face existential restructuring pressure, evidenced by the Intel breakup chatter. Watch **NVDA**, **INTC**, and **TSM** for how the Broadcom/TSMC speculation develops, plus **BABA** on follow-through from its new model launch. EA's buyout closing is a good marker of how much private capital — especially sovereign wealth money — is still willing to pay to take established tech and gaming franchises off public markets.
