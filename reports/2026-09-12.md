# 2026-09-12

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Oracle disclosed a record $664 billion AI cloud backlog** (remaining performance obligations), up $209 billion year-over-year, with more than $30 billion in new AI contracts booked last quarter and non-OpenAI backlog more than doubling — reinforcing Oracle as a key AI-infrastructure bellwether even as shares initially popped then gave back much of the gain on questions about financing structure and capex.
- **The Pentagon is in talks to lend roughly $5 billion to AI cloud startup Fluidstack** via its Office of Strategic Capital, aimed at shoring up U.S. data-center supply chains (transformers, switchgear, and other grid components) rather than funding compute directly — a first-of-its-kind government financing move into the AI infrastructure buildout, though terms are still unsettled.
- **Tencent-backed AI chipmaker Enflame surged as much as 179–220% on its Shanghai IPO debut**, raising about $912 million and closing with a roughly $25.5 billion market cap; it's the last of China's "four little dragons" of domestic AI chipmakers to go public, underscoring investor appetite for Nvidia alternatives amid China's chip self-sufficiency push.
- **Nvidia's $12.93 billion acquisition of Hugging Face moved into definitive-agreement territory** via SEC filing, cementing Nvidia's push to control the AI model-distribution layer (3M models, 18M+ developers) on top of its chip dominance; the deal is expected to close in H1 2027 pending regulatory approval, with Jensen Huang publicly pledging the platform will stay hardware-agnostic.
- **Holtec Nuclear priced terms for a Nasdaq IPO** (ticker HNUC), offering 50 million shares at $15–$18 to raise up to $900 million at a valuation near $10.2 billion, with pricing expected September 17 — another sign capital markets are betting on nuclear power as the bottleneck fix for AI data-center electricity demand.
- **Qualcomm's AI infrastructure tie-up with Amazon continued reverberating this week**: Qualcomm issued AWS warrants for 25 million shares (~$4 billion) as part of a multi-year custom AI inference silicon and interconnect partnership tied to a reported ~$60 billion hardware commitment from Amazon, keeping QCOM in focus as a chip-supplier winner in the hyperscaler AI buildout.

## What It Means

The dominant theme is that AI infrastructure financing is broadening beyond Big Tech balance sheets — Oracle's backlog shows demand is real but investors are scrutinizing how it gets funded, and the Pentagon stepping in with a multi-billion-dollar loan to a private AI cloud startup signals government capital is now part of the compute buildout story. On the equity side, watch ORCL for whether backlog-to-revenue conversion eases capex-payoff skepticism, QCOM and AMZN as the custom-silicon partnership matures, and NVDA as the Hugging Face deal works through regulatory review. IPO markets are clearly open for AI-adjacent plays — both a Chinese Nvidia rival (Enflame) and a nuclear power supplier (Holtec) found strong demand — which is worth tracking as a read on risk appetite heading into more tech listings this fall.
