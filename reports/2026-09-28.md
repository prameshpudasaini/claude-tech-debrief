# 2026-09-28

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Nvidia authorized a $150 billion increase to its stock buyback program**, lifting total repurchase capacity to $235 billion — the largest such increase in company history — while simultaneously launching an open AI-agent safety platform (OpenShell and Nvidia Sentry) with Anthropic, Microsoft, Cisco, Palantir, CrowdStrike, SpaceX and other partners.
- **Qualcomm and Amazon unveiled a multi-generational custom-silicon partnership**, with Amazon set to purchase up to $60 billion in AI data-center chips and related products from Qualcomm, deepening the chipmaker's push beyond mobile into AI infrastructure.
- **AMD acquired silicon-photonics startup Enosemi**, adding custom-materials technology aimed at accelerating co-packaged optics for AMD's next-generation AI systems.
- **Oura is entering its final IPO countdown**, pricing 50 million shares at $40–$44 to raise roughly $2.1 billion at a ~$15 billion fully diluted valuation, with its Nasdaq debut under ticker "OURA" expected September 30.
- **Anthropic is reportedly delaying its IPO until November**, as the company weighs a new frontier-model launch to compete with OpenAI's upcoming GPT-6 Astra before going to public markets.
- **GlobalFoundries and Renesas Electronics expanded their strategic chip-manufacturing collaboration**, giving Renesas broader access to GlobalFoundries' platforms for automotive and AI applications — the latest sign of semiconductor supply-chain consolidation.
- **Paramount Skydance's $110.9 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery is nearing its close**, after clearing a court-imposed deadline and finalizing a five-year antitrust consent decree, with completion expected within about two weeks.
- **Chip stocks diverged sharply in early trading**: Nvidia rose on its buyback and safety-platform news while Intel and Micron each fell more than 2%, and Meta slid roughly 3% premarket after last week's AI-agent-driven rally, as investors rotated out of richly valued AI names amid rising bond yields.

## What It Means

Today's news underscores two parallel currents in the AI trade: hyperscalers and chipmakers are still committing enormous capital to AI infrastructure and safety (Nvidia's record buyback plus its new agent-safety stack, Qualcomm's $60B Amazon silicon deal, AMD's photonics bolt-on), even as public markets show signs of fatigue — Meta and several semiconductor names sold off premarket despite the good news, and Anthropic pushed its IPO to November rather than compete with Oura's debut this week. Watch NVDA, QCOM, AMD and AMZN for continued capital-deployment headlines, and OURA on its Wednesday listing as a read on investor appetite for growth-stage tech debuts. The Qualcomm-Amazon and AMD-Enosemi deals both point to chipmakers racing to diversify beyond GPUs into custom silicon and photonics — a trend worth tracking alongside the ongoing Paramount-Warner Bros. Discovery close as a signal that large, complex M&A is still clearing regulatory hurdles even in a choppier market.
