# July 26, 2026 — Tech & Markets Morning Debrief

- **Oracle locks in a nearly $7B, 10-year Pentagon software deal.** The enterprise software agreement consolidates the Defense Department's on-premises Oracle licenses (covering DoD, the Coast Guard, and the intelligence community) into a single contract, following Microsoft's similar $9.69B DoD consolidation deal in May — a sign the Pentagon's software-spending overhaul is becoming a real, recurring revenue line for Big Tech vendors.
- **Combined Big Tech AI capex guidance for 2026 has climbed to roughly $725B (+77% YoY)**, with Meta raising its range to $125B–$145B, Microsoft to ~$190B, and Amazon to ~$200B — on top of Alphabet's $195B–$205B guidance that triggered a 7% stock plunge and Alphabet's first-ever negative free-cash-flow quarter. Wall Street is now openly demanding "receipts" on the spending ahead of this week's earnings.
- **Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon all report earnings this week (July 27–30)** — the next major test of whether hyperscaler AI infrastructure spending is converting into revenue, after Alphabet's mixed reaction set a cautious tone for the sector.
- **Tech's 2026 rally keeps stalling under capex anxiety**: the Nasdaq is on pace for a second straight weekly loss and the MSCI World Semiconductor Index is down 6% in July, as investors who drove the first-half AI rally turn skeptical that infrastructure spend is outpacing realized returns.
- **CuspAI, a Cambridge-based AI-for-materials-discovery startup, raised a $450M Series B at a $2.6B valuation** (up from $520M just nine months ago), co-led by Kleiner Perkins and NEA with Jeff Bezos and AMD Ventures participating — the startup is targeting the semiconductor industry's dependence on scarce rare metals like iridium and ruthenium, a supply-chain bottleneck increasingly relevant to AI chipmaking.

Today's dominant theme is capex fatigue: after a first half defined by ever-larger AI infrastructure bets (Nvidia/Samsung/SK Group's $700B+ in chip-supply pacts, Oracle and Microsoft's multibillion-dollar government contracts), investors are now pushing back hard on hyperscalers to show the spending is paying off, and the market's reaction to Alphabet's capex-driven selloff has set a nervous tone into this week's Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon reports. Watch **GOOGL, META, MSFT, and AMZN** most closely as earnings land July 27–30 — any further capex guidance hikes without matching revenue growth could extend the chip-sector slide, while **ORCL** stays in focus on its Pentagon contract wins and **AMD-adjacent** materials/supply-chain plays (like CuspAI) as a read on where AI infrastructure investment goes next.
