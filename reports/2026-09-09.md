# 2026-09-09

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Marvell's CEO called its custom AI silicon deal with Google "game-changing,"** saying it could generate as much as $120 billion in revenue through fiscal 2033 and make Google one of Marvell's largest investors via a stake worth up to $12.2 billion; Marvell also raised its FY2027 revenue outlook to ~$12 billion (from $11.5B) and FY2028 to ~$18 billion, though shares whipsawed — up sharply then giving back gains — on questions about customer concentration.
- **AI coding startup Cognition raised $2 billion in a new Series E at a $48 billion valuation**, nearly doubling its $26 billion mark from just four months ago; new investors Andreessen Horowitz and Accel joined existing backers Founders Fund, General Catalyst, and Avenir as Cognition's run-rate revenue grew from $492 million to almost $900 million since May.
- **Meta shares jumped as much as 6% after the company launched its "Muse" AI agent with paid subscription tiers**, the first concrete monetization move tied to its AI-agent push since July earnings — a signal investors are watching closely for whether Meta's AI spending is starting to convert into revenue.
- **AMD used Citi's Global TMT Conference to raise its total addressable AI market forecast to $3 trillion by 2030** (from a prior $2 trillion estimate), citing stronger-than-expected demand across GPUs, server CPUs, and system-level AI products; shares rose about 5.9% to roughly $505.
- **Alphabet fell about 2% as AI-spending anxiety and regulatory risk collided**: the slide followed Oracle's cloud-sales miss, which reignited market worries about the payoff on AI infrastructure capex, layered on top of reports that EU regulators are preparing a Digital Markets Act fine against Google and a U.S. appeals court allowing roughly 2,400 youth-safety lawsuits against Meta, Google, and Snap to proceed.

## What It Means

Today's moves show the AI trade splitting into "proof of monetization" winners and "show me the ROI" skeptics: Marvell and AMD are getting rewarded (with some volatility) for locking up custom-silicon supply and raising long-term TAM estimates, Meta popped on its first real AI-agent revenue signal, and Cognition's fresh $48B valuation shows private capital still chasing AI-coding-agent growth ahead of a probable IPO. On the other side, Alphabet's slide alongside Oracle's cloud-revenue miss is an early tell that investors are getting less patient with "trust us, the AI capex will pay off" narratives — a dynamic worth watching heading into Broadcom, Nvidia, and hyperscaler earnings later this quarter. Near-term, keep an eye on MRVL for signs the Google-deal jitters settle, META for whether Muse actually shows up in Q3 monetization numbers, and GOOGL/ORCL as bellwethers for whether the broader "AI infrastructure spend now, profit later" story is starting to wear thin with the market.
