# July 28, 2026 — Tech & Markets Morning Debrief

- **Nvidia is weighing a massive backstop for OpenAI**, reportedly discussing guaranteeing up to $250B in lease payments for a 10-gigawatt SoftBank-built Ohio data center and considering up to $350B in financing so OpenAI can buy Nvidia GPUs — reigniting Wall Street's "circular financing" concerns about AI infrastructure spending.
- **Chip stocks sold off sharply on the circular-financing worries**: Nvidia fell ~5%, AMD ~5.2%, SK Hynix ~7.5%, Sandisk ~11%, and Micron ~2.3%, even as the Dow rose ~1% and software stocks rallied (iShares Expanded Tech-Software ETF up almost 2%) — a growing split between hardware and software sentiment.
- **Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix unveiled $950B in combined memory-chip supply partnerships with U.S. tech giants**, with SK Hynix committing $750B in chips to companies including Nvidia and Samsung supplying $200B to Broadcom, locking in AI memory demand through 2030.
- **Nvidia signed a multi-year Arizona packaging deal with Amkor Technology**, including a $1.5B prepayment to secure advanced AI chip packaging capacity — Amkor shares jumped 12% after-hours on the news.
- **Nvidia and AMD will supply GPUs to Saudi Arabia's AI startup Humain**, with Nvidia deploying "several hundred thousand" advanced GPUs over five years, starting with an 18,000-GPU Grace Blackwell supercomputer — both chipmakers' shares moved higher on the announcement.
- **Big Tech earnings week is underway**, with Microsoft, Meta, and Apple among major reporters this week alongside a Fed policy decision — investors are watching for confirmation that hyperscaler AI capex is converting into revenue, a key backdrop to today's chip-sector jitters.

Today's dominant story is the tension between two competing AI narratives: mega-scale hardware supply deals (Samsung/SK Hynix's $950B pact, Nvidia's Amkor packaging deal, the Humain GPU order) show demand for AI infrastructure is still enormous, while Nvidia's potential $250B–$600B backstop of OpenAI has revived fears that much of that demand is being financed circularly by the same handful of companies. Watch **NVDA, AMD, MU, SNDK, and SK Hynix** for continued volatility, and **MSFT, META, and AAPL** as this week's earnings and the Fed decision will likely matter more for near-term direction than any single deal — a weak read on AI monetization could deepen today's chip selloff, while strong results could calm circular-financing fears.
