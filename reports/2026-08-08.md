# 2026-08-08

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals (last 24 hours)

- **AMD acquires AI inference chip startup Taalas.** AMD struck a definitive agreement to buy the Toronto-based startup, which "hardwires" AI models directly into silicon — its second Canadian AI-chip purchase in about a year and a fresh shot at closing the inference gap with Nvidia. Terms weren't disclosed.
- **Tesla, SpaceX, and Intel commit $16.8B to build "Terafab," a Texas chip mega-plant.** The Grimes County facility — over 100 million square feet, aimed at logic, memory, packaging, and testing for robots, robotaxis, and space-based data centers — is only phase one; SpaceX filings flag total spend could reach $119B.
- **Klaviyo (NYSE: KVYO) acquires AI startup Agency's team and technology.** The CRM platform is folding Agency's agentic customer-success tech into its Composer and Customer Agent products, with Agency co-founder Elias Torres joining as Chief Product Officer; terms undisclosed, close expected Q3.
- **Doosan agrees to buy a 70.6% stake in SK Siltron from SK Inc. for ~$1.7B.** The deal hands Doosan control of a major semiconductor wafer supplier, deepening its bet on chip-supply-chain exposure as memory and logic demand stays elevated.
- **Broadcom's (NASDAQ: AVGO) AI silicon backlog tops $230B.** Its ~$200B multiyear MOU with Samsung (memory, foundry, packaging) and $30B-plus custom-chipmaking commitment from Apple keep converting into firm orders, cementing Broadcom as the go-to custom-silicon partner for hyperscalers and device makers alike.
- **Retail-tech startup Radar raises $170M Series B at a ~$1B valuation.** The round funds Radar's in-store analytics platform for physical retailers, another data point on venture capital continuing to flow into AI-adjacent commerce tooling even as public-market attention stays fixed on chips.

## What it means

The pattern holding through this week: chipmakers and AI-hardware players keep converting the AI infrastructure boom into concrete, dollar-denominated commitments rather than vague roadmaps — AMD buying inference IP outright, Broadcom and Doosan locking down supply, and Tesla/SpaceX/Intel building physical fab capacity to reduce dependence on outside foundries. Watch AMD and INTC for reaction to the Terafab tie-up, AVGO as the Samsung and Apple orders move from MOU to backlog, and KVYO for how the Agency deal lands with investors watching its AI-agent story. Collectively, it signals Big Tech and chip suppliers are racing to secure both compute supply and specialized silicon talent before the next earnings cycle, a bullish near-term setup for the AI hardware chain but one where any capex wobble from a hyperscaler could hit sentiment hard.
