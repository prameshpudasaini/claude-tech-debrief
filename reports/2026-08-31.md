# 2026-08-31

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Nvidia is investing $3.5 billion in MediaTek convertible bonds**, deepening a partnership that spans AI data-center infrastructure, local AI computing (RTX Spark/DGX Spark chips) and automotive. MediaTek will adopt Nvidia's NVLink Fusion platform so hyperscalers and model developers can plug custom XPUs into Nvidia's rack-scale AI systems — one of Nvidia's largest direct investments outside the US. Watch NVDA and MediaTek (2454.TW) for follow-through.
- **Tim Cook stepped down as Apple's CEO today after 15 years**, handing the role to hardware chief John Ternus effective September 1; Cook stays on as executive chairman. It's the first CEO change at Apple since 2011, and closes out a run that took the company from roughly $350B to a $5T-plus market cap — a leadership-transition risk worth watching for AAPL in the near term.
- **PayPal's ~$53 billion buyout by a Stripe-led consortium with Advent International has collapsed.** Bloomberg reported Friday the group walked away after PayPal's stock re-rated higher, eroding the discount that made a leveraged deal attractive; PYPL fell as much as 12-16% and remains one of the market's biggest laggards this week. It's a reminder that even mega-cap fintech isn't immune to LBO math falling apart mid-negotiation.
- **Nvidia's reported $12.9 billion deal to buy Hugging Face is still unsigned but advancing**, per The Information and CNBC — a move that would extend Nvidia's reach from chips into the open-source AI model layer it doesn't already control. Any formal signing would be a fresh catalyst for NVDA.
- **Macro backdrop is weighing on tech:** the Nasdaq Composite slipped roughly 0.5% and the S&P 500 0.3% as fresh US-Iran strikes pushed oil prices higher and fed rate-hike bets, dragging AI-linked names down even as Info Tech (XLK) outperformed on the Nvidia/MediaTek news.

## What it means

Today's clearest signal is that AI infrastructure capital keeps flowing even as macro noise (oil-driven inflation fears, rate-hike speculation) rattles the broader tape — Nvidia's $3.5B MediaTek bet shows chipmakers still racing to lock in NVLink-ecosystem partners well beyond direct GPU sales. At the same time, two reminders landed that not every mega-deal survives contact with reality: PayPal's buyout imploded once the stock re-rated, and Apple just changed CEOs for the first time in 15 years, both events that inject company-specific uncertainty into names investors treat as bellwethers. Near-term, watch NVDA and MediaTek (2454.TW) for how the convertible-bond investment trades, AAPL for how markets price the Ternus transition, and PYPL for whether it finds a new suitor or trades as a standalone story now that the buyout is dead.
