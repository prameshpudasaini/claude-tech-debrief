# September 11, 2026

- **SpaceX discloses a $13 billion AI compute deal.** CFO Bret Johnsen revealed at Goldman Sachs' Communacopia + Technology conference that SpaceX signed a new AI hosting/compute agreement worth roughly $1.1 billion a month (about $13 billion annualized) with an undisclosed counterparty, payments starting in December. It's a big step toward SpaceX's stated $100 billion ARR target and underscores how deep the AI-compute land grab now runs beyond the traditional hyperscalers.
- **DOJ opens a formal antitrust probe into Nvidia's Groq licensing deal.** The Justice Department sent Nvidia a formal request for information over its ~$17-20 billion non-exclusive licensing deal with AI chip startup Groq (announced last December), examining whether the "reverse acquihire" structure — licensing tech and hiring key staff, including founder Jonathan Ross, without a formal merger — was designed to dodge antitrust review. It's the first real legal test of this increasingly common AI-industry deal structure.
- **Oracle lands a Pentagon software deal worth up to $6.99 billion.** Oracle secured a 10-year Department of War Enterprise Software Agreement covering on-prem software across the military branches, intelligence community, and Coast Guard. The win is seen as bullish for Oracle and, by extension, Palantir, Microsoft, and ServiceNow, all of which stand to benefit from expanded federal enterprise-software spending.
- **Anthropic walks away from its $6 billion Decart acquisition.** After months of talks that began in late May, Anthropic scrapped the planned purchase of AI video/media startup Decart, one of the larger AI acquisitions to collapse this year and a reminder that not every high-profile AI deal reaches the finish line.
- **Qualcomm-Amazon custom silicon pact continues to reverberate.** Their multi-generational agreement to co-develop custom AI inference chips and 1.6T optical interconnects for AWS — with warrants for Amazon to buy 25 million Qualcomm shares and up to $60 billion in potential future chip purchases — remains a top talking point this week, having already driven double-digit rallies in Qualcomm and lifted sentiment across custom-silicon suppliers.
- **DeepSeek lines up a Shanghai IPO.** The Chinese AI lab is preparing to go public on a mainland exchange, a notable step for a company at the center of the US-China AI competition narrative and one that could reshape sentiment around Chinese AI equities and US export-control debates.
- **Oura's Nasdaq IPO filing moves forward.** The wearables maker, which filed to list under ticker OURA on September 3, is still tracking toward a listing around the end of September — one of the more closely watched consumer-hardware debuts of the year.

Today's theme is AI infrastructure money continuing to flow at massive scale — SpaceX, Qualcomm/Amazon, and Oracle's government win all point to compute, custom silicon, and enterprise AI spending accelerating even as regulators start pushing back, with the DOJ's Nvidia-Groq probe signaling more scrutiny ahead for creative "acquihire-without-a-merger" deal structures. Watch NVDA for regulatory overhang, ORCL/PLTR/MSFT/NOW on the Pentagon contract halo effect, and QCOM for continued momentum on the Amazon tie-up. The Anthropic-Decart collapse and looming DeepSeek/Oura IPOs are also worth tracking as signals of where AI dealmaking and public-market appetite go next.
