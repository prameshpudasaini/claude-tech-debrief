# 2026-09-06

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Anthropic is set to publicly unveil its IPO prospectus right after Labor Day (Sept. 7), with a Nasdaq listing possible as soon as late September at a targeted valuation of $1.5–2 trillion** — which would make it the most valuable AI company to ever go public. The filing follows explosive growth: Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate reportedly hit about $65 billion by the end of July (up from roughly $9 billion at the end of 2025), and the company posted its first-ever quarterly operating profit in Q2.
- **TSMC raised its 2026 capital-expenditure guidance to $60–64 billion — roughly 90% above its year-end-2025 estimate — after AI-driven chipmaking-equipment demand nearly doubled to 1.9x its December baseline in just nine months.** The foundry also flagged an additional $100 billion Arizona investment and lifted its 2026 revenue-growth outlook above 40%, a sign AI capacity needs are outrunning even the world's largest chipmaker's build-out pace.
- **Micron shares are trading near all-time highs after the company confirmed its high-bandwidth memory (HBM) output is essentially sold out for all of 2026 under binding contracts**, giving it unusual revenue visibility. It's the clearest evidence yet that the AI memory shortage — also lifting Samsung and SK Hynix — remains one of the tightest, most profitable segments of the chip market.
- **Wearable-health company Oura formally filed its S-1 for a Nasdaq IPO under ticker OURA**, disclosing $1.21 billion in revenue for the nine months through June (up 74% year-over-year) and 5 million paid subscribers. Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan and a dozen other banks are underwriting a deal expected to price by late September — one of the largest consumer-tech listings of the year.
- **Global semiconductor stocks have shed roughly $3.3 trillion in combined market value since June 22 as investors grow more skeptical about near-term returns on AI infrastructure spending**, though chip names bounced off their lows to close last week as dip-buyers stepped back in. The gap between blowout capex/demand data (TSMC, Micron) and valuation fatigue looks set to keep AI-linked names volatile in the sessions ahead.

## What It Means

AI infrastructure demand still isn't slowing at the hardware level — TSMC and Micron are both effectively saying supply can't keep up — even as public investors grow warier about paying up for that growth story, a tension visible in the semiconductor sector's rough ten-week stretch despite the underlying data staying strong. The far bigger event to watch is Anthropic's imminent IPO prospectus: a $1.5–2 trillion debut would be the largest AI listing ever and could reset how the market prices OpenAI, xAI and other labs eyeing their own public offerings. Stocks to watch this week: TSM, MU, NVDA and AVGO on chip-capex headlines (with read-through to equipment makers like ASML and Applied Materials), plus Anthropic's SEC filing for the first real look at its financials and OURA's roadshow as a gauge of consumer-tech IPO appetite.
