# 2026-09-04

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **China's Moonshot AI confidentially filed for a Hong Kong IPO targeting roughly $3 billion at a $50 billion valuation**, working with Goldman Sachs, CICC, and Deutsche Bank — one of the largest Chinese AI listings in the pipeline and a reminder that global AI competition (and capital) isn't confined to U.S. players like OpenAI and Anthropic.
- **Crusoe, the AI cloud/data-center developer that supplies GPU capacity to OpenAI, Microsoft, and Meta, finalized a $3 billion-plus funding round at a roughly $30 billion valuation** (triple its valuation from last October), led by Atreides Management and Valor Equity Partners with Mubadala Capital participating — underscoring how much private capital is still chasing AI infrastructure buildout.
- **Andreessen Horowitz-backed Gimlet Labs raised $300 million at a $3 billion valuation, with new backing from Arm Holdings and Microsoft's M12 venture fund.** Gimlet's software routes AI inference across multiple chip vendors rather than locking workloads to one supplier — a signal that hyperscalers are actively investing in ways to reduce dependence on Nvidia.
- **Snowflake shares surged roughly 22% after a blowout fiscal Q2 report** (EPS of $0.62 vs. $0.45 expected, revenue up 35% to $1.55 billion), driven by enterprise adoption of its CoCo AI coding agent; a wave of sell-side price-target hikes followed from Jefferies, TD Cowen, Truist, and Deutsche Bank.
- **Tesla stock fell more than 5% after federal regulators (NHTSA) opened a safety probe into its steering-wheel-free Cybercabs**, just a day after the driverless vehicles began carrying riders on Austin streets — regulators are questioning whether Tesla's self-certification actually meets federal vehicle-safety standards.

## What It Means

AI infrastructure spending is still the market's dominant story, but the flavor is shifting from "buy compute" to "diversify and hedge": Crusoe's raise shows data-center capacity is still scarce and richly valued, while Gimlet Labs' round — backed by Arm and Microsoft — signals real hyperscaler appetite for reducing reliance on any single chipmaker, Nvidia included. Snowflake's post-earnings pop is worth watching as a bellwether for whether AI tooling is genuinely converting into enterprise software revenue, which could lift other data/analytics names (Datadog, MongoDB) on similar read-throughs. Moonshot AI's Hong Kong IPO filing is a reminder that Chinese AI labs are racing toward public markets on a similar timeline to U.S. names, adding a competitive-capital dimension worth tracking. On the risk side, Tesla's Cybercab probe is a near-term overhang on TSLA and a signal that autonomous-vehicle regulatory risk hasn't gone away even as the technology rolls out faster — keep an eye on NHTSA developments there and on SNOW, AI-infra names, and chip stocks (NVDA, AMD, ARM) heading into next week.
