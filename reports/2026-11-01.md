# 2026-11-01

*Saturday edition: markets are closed and few new deals dropped in the last 24 hours, so this covers the biggest announcements from the past several days that markets will react to Monday.*

- **Nvidia and AMD invest $4B in Meta:** Nvidia is putting in $3B and AMD $1B as part of Meta's planned $95B raise for AI infrastructure. Meta will expand its use of Nvidia's Vera Rubin systems and adopt a custom AMD Instinct-based accelerator later.
- **DoorDash to buy Instacart for ~$17B:** DoorDash agreed to pay $65 per share (a 26% premium, at least 75% cash), backed by a JPMorgan bridge loan. Closing is expected late 2027 to 1H 2028, pending antitrust review.
- **Intel to acquire quantum-software firm Classiq:** About $1.9B upfront plus up to $350M in earn-outs (press reports say ~$2.3B). It is the largest quantum-computing acquisition to date; closing is expected in 1H 2027.
- **Guidewire to buy hyperexponential:** The insurance-software maker will pay $1.7B in cash plus up to $400M tied to recurring-revenue targets.
- **Anthropic IPO still the big overhang:** Anthropic filed a confidential S-1 in June at a $965B valuation; reports point to a listing before year-end, possibly mid-November. No public S-1 or pricing is confirmed, and valuation figures ($1.5T–$2T) from secondary outlets are unverified.
- **SPAC deal in crypto mining:** Starlink AI Acquisition Corp. (NYSE: OTAI) signed a business combination with Spider Digital, a crypto mining pool operator. Small and speculative.

## Takeaway

AI spending is the theme. Chipmakers are now buying stakes in their own customers, which locks in demand for Nvidia and AMD but adds "circular deal" bubble worries. Watch NVDA, AMD and META on Monday, plus DASH and CART (Instacart likely trades near the $65 offer) and INTC for quantum exposure. The Anthropic IPO could pull money from other AI and growth stocks if it lands in November.

*Sources: CNBC, SiliconANGLE, SEC filings (DoorDash 8-K/10-Q, Intel 10-Q), Quantum Computing Report, Renaissance Capital, Globe Newswire. Some figures come from secondary outlets.*
