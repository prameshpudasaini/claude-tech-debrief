# 2026-09-03

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Nvidia confirmed it is acquiring Hugging Face, the open-source AI model-sharing platform, for roughly $12.9 billion.** The deal, which Nvidia says will keep Hugging Face "open" for the broader AI ecosystem, is expected to close in the first half of 2027 pending regulatory review — and deepens Nvidia's reach into AI developer tooling even as key customers like Meta, OpenAI, and Microsoft build out their own compute.
- **Broadcom's fiscal Q3 report landed with AI semiconductor revenue up 221% year-over-year to $16.7 billion, but Q4 guidance of $34.8 billion came in just under the Street's ~$35 billion target.** Shares fell about 6% despite the beat, and the reaction rippled into Nvidia and AMD given Broadcom's custom-chip roster of Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta.
- **Palantir jumped 8.7% after subsidiary Palantir USG landed a U.S. Army prime contractor agreement to build and deliver eight TITAN ground-station systems**, reinforcing the company's expanding defense-tech contract pipeline as a stock catalyst.
- **Enterprise AI startup Wonderful closed a $550 million Series C at a $5 billion valuation (more than double its prior round), with Salesforce joining as a first-time investor** alongside lead Insight Partners and returning backers Index Ventures, IVP, and Bessemer — a notable strategic bet by a public SaaS incumbent into the "AI operating system" space.
- **Smart-ring maker Oura is reportedly pressing ahead with a September IPO that could raise up to $3 billion at a valuation above $16 billion**, with Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and JPMorgan lined up as underwriters — one of the most closely watched consumer-tech listings still in the pipeline.

## What It Means

AI infrastructure remains the dominant through-line: Nvidia's move to buy Hugging Face shows the big compute players still racing to own more of the AI developer stack, while Broadcom's beat-but-underwhelming guidance is a reminder that investors are getting pickier about whether AI capex momentum can keep beating already-elevated expectations — watch NVDA, AVGO, and AMD for continued swings on this theme. Palantir's fresh defense win keeps it in focus as a government-AI trade separate from the hyperscaler capex story. Meanwhile, private markets are still flush: Wonderful's Salesforce-backed round and Oura's advancing IPO plans both point to capital continuing to chase AI and health-tech growth stories, which could feed into more strategic M&A or a reopening IPO window in the weeks ahead.
