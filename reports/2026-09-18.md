# 2026-09-18

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Anthropic and OpenAI are both quietly hunting for smaller AI data center deals** — sources told CNBC the two labs are chasing 20–30 MW deployments in the US, UK, and Nordics (versus their usual multi-hundred-megawatt/gigawatt megadeals), prioritizing "speed to usable capacity" over scale as the compute race intensifies; a signal that smaller data-center operators and regional power/colocation names could see fresh deal flow.
- **Google, Nvidia, and startup Emerald AI launched the AI Energy Management Alliance**, a coalition that wants AI data centers to dynamically flex their grid draw instead of acting as static loads; the group launched with 18+ partners including Anthropic, chipmaker Analog Devices, and utilities National Grid, Constellation, AES, RWE, and NRG, and claims the approach could theoretically unlock up to 100 GW of additional data-center capacity — a potential tailwind for grid-tech and utility names tied to AI power demand.
- **Apple is reportedly in talks with rival Nvidia to license NVLink Fusion** for a prospective rack-mounted "M8 Ultra" AI server line aimed at developers, businesses, and governments; no product would ship before 2029 and nothing is finalized, but the talks mark a notable thaw between two companies that rarely partner on core silicon — worth watching for both AAPL and NVDA.
- **Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said Thursday that Nvidia expects to sell twice as many chips next year**, citing AI demand "in almost every single country," a comment that pushed NVDA shares up roughly 2% and reinforced the company's prior guidance of ~70% revenue growth (to about $673B) for the fiscal year ending January 2028.
- **Nvidia's $12.9 billion acquisition of Hugging Face** continues to reverberate as the company's second-largest deal ever (after its $20B Groq-assets purchase); roughly $11.9B goes to shareholders with up to $1B in retention equity for employees, extending Nvidia's push from chips into the open-source AI software stack.
- **Mistral AI closed a €3 billion ($3.5B) Series D led by Samsung Electronics**, valuing the French AI lab at more than €21 billion in what Mistral calls the largest all-equity round ever closed by a European tech company — another sign of strategic corporate money (not just VCs) chasing frontier AI stakes.

## What It Means

The throughline today is the AI compute arms race maturing past "buy more gigawatts" into smarter, faster, and stranger deal structures: labs are now chasing smaller, quicker-to-build data centers instead of only megaprojects, hyperscalers are teaming with utilities to squeeze more capacity out of the existing grid, and even Apple and Nvidia — longtime rivals — are reportedly discussing a silicon partnership to get AI servers built faster. Nvidia remains the hub of nearly every thread (Hugging Face, the energy alliance, the Apple talks, and Huang's bullish chip-sales guidance), keeping NVDA the single most important ticker to watch into next week. Also worth tracking: AAPL on any confirmation of NVLink Fusion talks, utility/grid-tech names (Constellation, AES, NRG) as AI-power partners, and Samsung (Mistral's new anchor investor) for its growing AI-equity portfolio. With chip demand guidance still accelerating and power/data-center speed becoming the new competitive edge, near-term sentiment favors AI-infrastructure and semiconductor names, though power-grid capacity remains the bottleneck everyone is now racing to solve.
