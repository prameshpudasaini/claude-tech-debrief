# July 29, 2026 — Tech & Markets Morning Debrief

- **South Korea's AI chip supply wave keeps expanding**: Samsung Electronics locked in a five-year, $200B-plus partnership with Broadcom pairing Broadcom's custom AI silicon designs with Samsung's sub-2nm foundry and joint HBM4E/HBM5 memory development — a direct swing at TSMC's foundry dominance and a bullish signal for Samsung's (SSNLF) and Broadcom's (AVGO) AI roadmaps.
- **SK Hynix and SK Group detailed roughly $750B in long-term memory-supply agreements** with Nvidia and other major tech buyers, part of a combined ~$950B in Korean semiconductor commitments unveiled this week — reinforcing that AI memory demand is contracted out years in advance even as chip stocks have been volatile on capex-circularity worries.
- **Micron agreed to buy a Taiwan chip-fabrication site from Powerchip Semiconductor (PSMC) for $1.8B in cash**, adding DRAM capacity ahead of a phased output ramp starting in 2027 — a direct bet that AI-driven memory demand outlasts near-term pricing swings. (MU)
- **TPG is reportedly evaluating a roughly $3B acquisition of Netrality Data Centers**, the latest private-equity move chasing AI-driven data-center real estate as hyperscalers keep outsourcing capacity buildout.
- **S&P Global signed a definitive agreement to acquire datacenterHawk**, a data-center and fiber-infrastructure intelligence provider, adding proprietary data-center analytics to its business alongside a separate agreed deal for a majority stake in Pan-African rating agency Agusto & Co. (SPGI)
- **Big Tech's "earnings vs. capex" reckoning hits a peak today**: Alphabet's report last week triggered a roughly 7% slide in Google shares and pulled Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft down with it on AI-spending scrutiny, and now Meta, Microsoft, and Apple all report earnings today — the same day the Fed opens its two-day policy meeting under Chair Kevin Warsh. (GOOGL, META, MSFT, AAPL)
- **AMD's new Helios AI server rack has added Microsoft as a customer**, joining Meta, OpenAI, and Oracle — a concrete data point that hyperscalers are diversifying AI compute purchases beyond Nvidia. (AMD, MSFT)
- **Nvidia continues seeding the AI startup ecosystem it depends on**, with a stake in Safe Superintelligence and participation in a $60M round for chip-design startup ChipAgents — reinforcing Nvidia's strategy of funding the demand side of its own supply chain. (NVDA)

Today's dominant theme is a split-screen AI narrative: on one side, the semiconductor supply chain keeps signing eye-popping, multi-year contracts (Samsung-Broadcom's $200B pact, SK Hynix's $750B Nvidia-linked commitments, Micron's Taiwan fab buy) that assume AI infrastructure demand keeps compounding for years. On the other, investors are growing warier of the bill — Alphabet's post-earnings selloff dragged the other hyperscalers down, and today's Meta/Microsoft/Apple earnings plus a Fed decision will likely set the market's near-term tone more than any single deal. Watch **MU, AVGO, SSNLF-equivalents, and AMD** for supply-chain-driven moves, and **GOOGL, META, MSFT, and AAPL** for capex commentary that could either calm or reignite the "circular AI financing" worries that have hit chip and hyperscaler stocks this week.
