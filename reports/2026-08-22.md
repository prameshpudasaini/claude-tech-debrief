# 2026-08-22

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Nvidia strikes a $7B combo deal with AI startup Poolside.** Nvidia is paying $6B for a non-exclusive license to Poolside's "Model Factory" (its platform for generating coding-focused AI models) and separately investing $1B in the startup at a $12B pre-money valuation. Nvidia is also extending job offers to roughly 109 Poolside employees, while the founders keep the company independent — NVDA stock still ended last week about 5% lower as investors weighed the price tag.
- **Nvidia is now circling a second chip startup: Korea's Rebellions.** Jensen Huang met Rebellions' CEO at Nvidia's Santa Clara HQ to discuss a possible technical partnership, investment, or outright acquisition. Rebellions builds energy-efficient NPUs for AI inference and is valued around $2.3B, backed by SK Hynix, Samsung Ventures and Arm — any acquisition would likely draw antitrust scrutiny in the US, EU and South Korea given Seoul's treatment of chips as a strategic asset.
- **Broadcom is lining up one of the largest AI debt deals ever: $70–80B.** The financing is split into a roughly $45B senior tranche and $35B junior tranche, and the capital is expected to help fund AI buildout including for Anthropic. It follows AMD's $4.75B bond sale earlier this month, adding to an AI-linked debt wave Goldman estimates could hit $300–570B in 2026.
- **Anthropic is preparing an IPO that could beat SpaceX's record.** The Claude maker is reportedly targeting a valuation north of $2 trillion, finalizing a multi-billion-dollar credit facility, and evaluating super-voting shares to keep founder control — with a filing that could land as early as late August, potentially putting it public before rival OpenAI.
- **Tech stocks had a rough week, but not uniformly.** The S&P 500 tech sector shed over 3% for the week; Amkor Technology and Credo Technology each fell roughly 11–15%, and Meta slid nearly 7%. Tesla was the standout gainer, on pace for its best week since May on robotaxi-related developments, while crypto-linked names (Robinhood, Coinbase) rallied alongside a 22% weekly bitcoin advance.
- **Alibaba is shopping its videogame business for at least $1.5B**, continuing a trend of Chinese tech conglomerates shedding non-core units to focus capital on AI infrastructure.

## What it means

The chip and AI-infrastructure supply chain keeps fragmenting: Nvidia is simultaneously licensing/hiring its way into Poolside and exploring a deal with Rebellions, a hedge against depending on any single model or inference-chip source. At the same time, the AI buildout is increasingly debt-financed rather than equity-financed — Broadcom's potential $70–80B raise and AMD's recent bond sale show chipmakers tapping credit markets at a pace that's starting to worry bond traders about hidden leverage. Anthropic's SpaceX-beating IPO ambitions signal AI labs think public markets are now ready to underwrite trillion-dollar-plus valuations, which could reset the bar for OpenAI's eventual listing. Near-term, watch NVDA (earnings next week), AVGO and AMD on debt-market reaction, and SOUN/robotics-adjacent names for spillover sentiment — while Meta's weekly slide is worth tracking for a possible pause in AI-capex enthusiasm.
