# 2026-09-21

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Smart-ring maker Oura set terms for its Nasdaq IPO**, offering 50 million shares at $40–$44 each to raise up to roughly $2.2 billion at a market cap near $13.5 billion; Eli Lilly indicated interest in buying up to $100 million of shares and Dragoneer up to $300 million, with Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and J.P. Morgan leading the offering under ticker OURA.
- **Anthropic's IPO timeline slipped again, now targeting a November listing** rather than October, as the company discusses raising as much as $100 billion at a valuation around $2 trillion — which would top SpaceX's record raise; Nvidia is reportedly in talks to make a cornerstone investment of up to $10 billion in the offering.
- **Taboola (Nasdaq: TBLA) made a formal offer to acquire Dianomi**, a London-based finance and business ad network, at up to 88 pence per share (roughly £27 million total); over 75% of Dianomi shareholders have already agreed, and the deal extends Taboola's push into higher-value financial-services advertising.
- **AI infrastructure builder Crusoe closed the initial tranche of a $3.9 billion Series F at a $30.9 billion valuation**, with Nvidia and Salesforce Ventures joining a roster of new and existing backers; Crusoe says it now has more than $140 billion in total contracted value and over 6 gigawatts of contracted data center capacity, underscoring how much public-company capital (Nvidia's chief among it) keeps flowing into AI compute buildouts.
- **Apple's EU merger filings confirmed its acquisition of Sonera**, a startup building chip-based magnetic sensors that could enable non-invasive health and movement tracking — the latest in a string of Apple tuck-ins as it works to bolster wearables and on-device AI hardware.
- **Nvidia remains in early talks to invest in or acquire South Korean AI inference-chip startup Rebellions**, valued near $2.3 billion, following a meeting between CEO Jensen Huang and Rebellions' founder at Nvidia's Santa Clara headquarters — part of Nvidia's broader pattern of licensing, investing in, or absorbing smaller chip and AI-model startups (it struck a similar $6 billion licensing-plus-investment deal with coding startup Poolside last month).

## What It Means

The through-line remains the AI-infrastructure capital cycle: Nvidia keeps showing up on both sides of the ledger, financing the labs and clouds it depends on (Crusoe, a possible Anthropic cornerstone stake) while also snapping up smaller chip and model talent (Poolside, and potentially Rebellions) — a pattern that keeps NVDA sentiment tightly linked to the health of the broader AI-funding market. On the public markets side, Oura's pricing gives traders a live AI-adjacent hardware IPO to watch into its debut this week, while Anthropic's slipping timeline is a reminder that even the most in-demand AI listing is still negotiating scale and backers rather than a lock for October. Apple's steady drumbeat of small tuck-in acquisitions (Sonera following July's AI-chip shopping reports) signals it's still playing infrastructure catch-up rather than making a single large move — worth watching for whether that turns into a bigger chip-startup deal. Smaller-cap ad-tech consolidation (Taboola-Dianomi) is a lower-stakes but real stock mover for TBLA holders, showing the M&A appetite extends beyond AI into adjacent digital-advertising niches.
