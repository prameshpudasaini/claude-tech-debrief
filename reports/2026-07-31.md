# July 31, 2026 — Tech & Markets Morning Debrief

- **Amazon's Q2 print blew past every estimate**, with AWS growing 36.7% — its fastest pace in 18 quarters — and both its AI and chips businesses topping $25B annualized run-rates; shares surged as much as 13% in premarket trading, the biggest single mover of the week. Results were further inflated by roughly $53B in non-operating gains tied to Amazon's Anthropic stake, and management raised 2026 capex guidance to $220B.
- **Apple's Q3 report was the week's disappointment**, with Services revenue and China sales both missing estimates — shares tumbled about 7%, underscoring how the market is penalizing Apple's more conservative, rent-don't-build approach to AI infrastructure compared to peers now showing AI revenue payoff.
- **Qualcomm closed its roughly $4B all-stock acquisition of Modular**, the AI-infrastructure startup behind the Mojo programming language, on July 29 — founder Chris Lattner becomes EVP of advanced AI software and platforms, extending Qualcomm's push from mobile chips into AI software and data-center compute.
- **Alphabet's stake in Anthropic is now worth roughly $124B**, per its latest quarterly filing — up sharply from the up-to-$40B it pledged to invest — alongside a $94B stake in SpaceX. The disclosure helped drive Alphabet's biggest-ever profit quarter and shows how much of Big Tech's earnings growth is now flowing through paper gains on AI startup bets rather than core operations.
- **AI-focused hedge fund Situational Awareness lost 67% of its value in July** after dumping large positions in SK Hynix, SanDisk, and Nebius amid margin calls — a sign that AI-adjacent memory and chip trades have gotten crowded and volatile enough to blow up leveraged funds.
- **Treasury yields kept climbing**, with the 10-year hitting 4.74% intraday — its highest since January 2025 — adding a valuation headwind for high-multiple tech and AI names even as single-stock earnings reactions dominated the tape.

This is Big Tech earnings week's final verdict, and it's splitting hard along one line: companies showing AI spend converting into revenue (Amazon, Microsoft earlier this week) are being rewarded, while those seen as spending without proof of payoff or pulling back (Apple, and Meta earlier this week) are getting sold. Watch **AMZN** for continued strength, **AAPL** for further downside follow-through, and **QCOM** as it integrates Modular into its AI/data-center strategy. The Situational Awareness blowup and rising yields are also worth tracking — both point to fragile positioning underneath the AI trade that could amplify moves in **SK Hynix, SanDisk, and Nebius** if sentiment sours further.
