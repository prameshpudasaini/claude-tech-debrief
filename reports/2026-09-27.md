# 2026-09-27

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Akamai landed an $11.6 billion, seven-year cloud infrastructure deal with Anthropic** (with room to expand to roughly $20 billion), sending Akamai shares up as much as 20% after-hours — Anthropic also received warrants for up to ~5% of Akamai's stock, and the news lifted rival neoclouds CoreWeave and Cloudflare too.
- **Nscale raised $3.36 billion in convertible financing ahead of its NYSE IPO** (ticker NSCL, expected mid-October), with its filing disclosing $103.4 billion in contracted AI-infrastructure business — including a $45 billion capacity deal with Anthropic and a $43.8 billion GPU-supply agreement with Microsoft.
- **Microsoft committed more than $10 billion through 2030 to a new Middle East AI and cloud framework** covering Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar and Kuwait, deepening its regional data-center and compute footprint as hyperscalers race to lock up power and capacity.
- **Paramount Skydance cleared a major hurdle in its $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery**, settling antitrust claims with 12 states and the Writers Guild of America; both stocks rallied on the news, and Paramount's CEO said the deal could close within about two weeks.
- **Oura is set to debut on the Nasdaq this week under ticker OURA**, pricing its IPO at $40–$44/share for a fully diluted valuation of roughly $15.6 billion; Eli Lilly has signaled interest in buying up to $100 million of shares, underscoring pharma's growing appetite for wearable health data.
- **AI-driven chip demand kept pushing semiconductor stocks higher into the weekend**, with AMD closing above a $1 trillion market cap and Intel and Marvell posting double-digit weekly gains, as investors bet that AI "agent" workloads (following Meta's fast-growing Muse app) will need more general-purpose compute, not just accelerators.

## What It Means

The theme this week is AI infrastructure spending flowing directly into the balance sheets of "picks-and-shovels" providers rather than just the model makers: Akamai's $11.6B Anthropic deal and Nscale's IPO disclosures (with its own multibillion-dollar Anthropic and Microsoft contracts) show cloud and neocloud names capturing outsized re-ratings as AI capacity gets locked up years in advance — watch AKAM, NSCL (once listed), CoreWeave and Cloudflare for follow-through. Microsoft's Middle East buildout signals hyperscalers are now geographically diversifying compute at the same pace they're expanding capacity domestically. In media, the Paramount-Warner Bros. Discovery settlement removes the last major roadblock to one of the year's largest deals, making PSKY and WBD near-term event-driven trades into a likely close. Oura's Nasdaq debut this week is the next big test of whether public markets will still pay growth multiples for consumer hardware/health-data platforms, while the broadening chip rally suggests traders are rotating into second-tier semiconductor names (AMD, Intel, Marvell) on the thesis that AI agents will drive a new wave of CPU and general-compute demand alongside GPUs.
