# 2026-08-16

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Silver Lake in talks to take Workday private (~$43B).** Reuters reported the private-equity firm has been in months-long discussions to buy the HR/finance-software maker, which would rank among the largest-ever software buyouts. Workday shares spiked as much as 25% on the news before a trading halt, briefly pushing its market cap above $50B — one of the biggest single-stock moves of the month.
- **Intel upsizes its stock offering to $20B to fund AI buildout.** Intel priced the enlarged common-stock sale at $95/share, drawing roughly $100B in institutional orders (5x oversubscribed). Proceeds are earmarked for AI chip production capacity, foundry expansion and next-gen manufacturing — the stock has held above the offer price, signaling investors are shrugging off dilution risk.
- **Nielsen to acquire DoubleVerify for $2.15B in cash.** Nielsen will pay $13.60/share (a 30% premium) for the ad-verification firm, financed via debt from Barclays, BofA and Citi plus equity and cash on hand. The combined ad-measurement platform would generate over $4B in annual revenue; the deal is expected to close by Q1 2027, pending DV shareholder and regulatory approval.
- **Databricks raises $5B at a $190B valuation.** The data/AI platform settled between a $1B ask from the company and a $15B push from eager investors, underscoring how much capital is still chasing the leading private AI infrastructure names ahead of an eventual IPO.
- **Cognition (Devin's maker) in talks for a $40B valuation.** Less than three months after raising $1B at $26B, the AI-coding startup is fielding new funding talks as its annualized revenue run rate approaches $1B — a signal that AI-coding-agent economics are scaling fast enough to justify another 50%+ valuation jump.
- **Samsung and SK hynix cement $950B in AI chip supply deals.** The two memory giants converted existing contracts with Broadcom, Nvidia and Microsoft into five-year agreements — Samsung/Broadcom covering $200B+ in advanced memory and foundry cooperation, SK Group committing $750B in memory supply to Nvidia and others — locking in AI hardware supply chains for the rest of the decade.

## What it means

The through-line today is capital chasing two things at once: take-private and buyout appetite for established enterprise software (Workday, DoubleVerify) at rich premiums, and continued mega-round investment in AI infrastructure and coding platforms (Databricks, Cognition) even as valuations climb into the tens of billions. Intel's oversubscribed raise and the Samsung/SK hynix supply-chain lock-in show chip and memory suppliers are still the preferred way for investors to play the AI buildout with less speculative risk than pure model-layer bets. Traders should watch WDAY for deal-confirmation headlines (a formal agreement would be a major software-sector catalyst), INTC as the AI-capex raise gets put to work, and DV/memory names (MU, SK Hynix-linked suppliers) on ripple effects from the Nielsen and Samsung deals.
