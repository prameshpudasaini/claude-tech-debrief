# 2026-08-18

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Nvidia backs OpenAI's Ohio data center with up to $105B.** Per an SEC filing Monday, Nvidia will guarantee financing for SB Energy's PORTS-Pike Technology Campus (initial 4.25GW, expandable to 8GW) under a 20-year OpenAI lease, plus a $1.5B direct investment in SB Energy. It's a scaled-down version of the previously floated $250B backstop, and keeps circular-financing questions (Nvidia funding the buildout that fuels demand for its own chips) front and center for investors.
- **TTM Technologies buys Epiq Solutions from Veritas Capital for $1.1B.** The deal adds Epiq's software-defined radio and embedded-computing tech — used across commercial, government and defense customers — to TTM's portfolio; expected to close by year-end pending regulatory approval.
- **Wispr raises $280M Series B at a $2B valuation.** The AI-dictation startup is pushing beyond speech-to-text into broader ambient AI tools, the latest sign VCs are still writing large checks for consumer-facing AI apps even as infrastructure valuations get scrutinized.
- **Chip stocks sell off broadly; Nasdaq down ~1%, Philadelphia Semiconductor Index off ~3.7%.** Nvidia and Meta led tech lower amid renewed "circular financing" jitters tied to the OpenAI/Nvidia Ohio deal, plus lingering fallout from reports that Meta is evaluating Google TPUs for its 2027 buildout — a potential crack in Nvidia's near-monopoly on AI accelerators.
- **Meta-Google TPU talks continue to weigh on Nvidia sentiment.** Reports that Meta may deploy Google's custom TPUs in its data centers starting 2027 (and rent TPU capacity from Google Cloud as soon as next year) keep resurfacing as a swing factor for NVDA shares, since even a partial shift from a buyer of Meta's size would validate a credible second source for AI chips.
- **Nvidia's $500B Wall Street financing platform (Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, KKR) remains in focus.** First unveiled August 10, the memoranda of understanding to mobilize $500B+ in third-party capital for AI compute buildout are still being finalized — no partner has disclosed a dollar commitment yet, but the structure underscores how deeply traditional finance is now underwriting the AI infrastructure race.

## What it means

The dominant theme remains Nvidia's expanding role as financier-in-chief for AI infrastructure — backstopping OpenAI's Ohio campus, lining up $500B from Wall Street, and investing directly in data-center developers — which keeps fueling "circular financing" worries that show up directly in today's chip-stock selloff. At the same time, the Meta/Google TPU story is the more structural risk to watch: any real diversification away from Nvidia GPUs by a hyperscaler-scale buyer would be a meaningful multi-year shift, not just a headline. Traders should watch NVDA and the SOX index for follow-through on both stories, META for confirmation of a TPU deployment timeline, and smaller-cap AI/consumer names like Wispr's peers for signs of whether venture appetite is holding up even as public AI infrastructure stocks wobble.
