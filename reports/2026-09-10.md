# September 10, 2026

- **Qualcomm and Amazon strike major AI chip deal.** Qualcomm and AWS announced a multi-generational partnership to co-develop custom AI inference silicon and next-gen optical interconnects for AWS data centers, with Amazon committing to buy up to $60 billion in Qualcomm server chips and related tech over time. Qualcomm also issued Amazon warrants to buy 25 million shares (~$4 billion) as part of the deal, and QCOM stock jumped as much as 9-10% on the news.
- **Corning inks multi-billion-dollar fiber deal with Verizon.** Corning signed a multi-year fiber-optic supply agreement with Verizon running 2027–2032, covering over 80 million miles of high-density fiber to support U.S. broadband and AI data-center buildouts. Corning shares surged roughly 8% on the announcement.
- **AI infrastructure stocks rally broadly on the Qualcomm/Corning news.** The pair of deals lifted a wide swath of chip and infrastructure names: Intel and AMD gained 9% and 6% respectively, Hewlett Packard Enterprise rose 8%, and photonics suppliers Lumentum (+11%), Coherent (+7%), and AXT (+12%) all rallied on renewed confidence that AI data-center spending remains strong.
- **Nvidia closes its $12.93 billion acquisition of Hugging Face.** Nvidia's deal to buy the leading open-source AI model-sharing platform — its second-largest acquisition ever, after the Groq asset purchase — became definitive this week, structured as roughly $11.9 billion cash plus up to $1 billion in staff equity retention. The deal extends Nvidia's reach from chips into the software layer where AI models are discovered and deployed.
- **AMD lifts its long-term AI chip market forecast.** AMD's CFO raised the company's outlook for the total addressable AI chip market toward $3 trillion, reinforcing bullish sentiment across the semiconductor sector and pushing AMD shares up roughly 3% even without a new deal announcement.

Today's moves all point the same direction: hyperscalers and chipmakers are locking in multi-year, multi-billion-dollar supply and infrastructure commitments to secure AI compute capacity, and Wall Street is rewarding every name tied to that build-out — chips (Qualcomm, Intel, AMD), networking/photonics (Corning, Lumentum, Coherent, AXT), and AI software distribution (Nvidia/Hugging Face). Watch QCOM, GLW, INTC, AMD, and the smaller photonics names (LITE, COHR, AXTI) for continued momentum, since custom-silicon and optical-networking deals are emerging as the next leg of the AI infrastructure trade beyond pure GPU demand. The Qualcomm-Amazon tie-up in particular signals hyperscalers diversifying AI chip supply beyond Nvidia, a trend worth tracking for its competitive impact on Nvidia's data-center dominance.
