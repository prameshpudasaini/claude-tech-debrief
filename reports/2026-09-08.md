# 2026-09-08

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Qualcomm struck a multi-generational AI chip partnership with Amazon Web Services worth up to $60 billion**, under which AWS will buy Qualcomm's custom AI inference accelerators and co-develop high-speed optical interconnects (up to 1.6 Tbps) for hyperscale data centers; Qualcomm also issued Amazon a warrant to buy roughly $4 billion of QCOM stock at $161.26/share. Shares jumped as much as 9–10% on the news, QCOM's biggest one-day move of the year.
- **Oura Health's Nasdaq IPO (ticker OURA) is progressing toward pricing**, with the smart-ring maker targeting a raise of up to $3 billion at a valuation north of $16 billion; the S-1 shows $1.4 billion in trailing revenue (+74% nine-month growth) and 5 million paid subscribers, with Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and J.P. Morgan leading the book.
- **Anthropic's IPO timeline slipped again**, with the prospectus filing now expected in late September and marketing to begin mid-October — just before the U.S. midterms — as the company targets a valuation as high as $2 trillion, which would make it the largest tech listing ever.
- **Broader indices split on the day**: the Dow and S&P 500 fell on rising oil prices and an escalating U.S.-Canada trade dispute, while the Nasdaq outperformed on relative strength in semiconductor stocks, underscoring how AI-chip names are currently insulated from macro headline risk.
- **Custom AI silicon (ASIC) makers stayed in focus** after Broadcom's early-September results reiterated fiscal 2027 AI revenue guidance above $100 billion on multi-gigawatt commitments from Google, Meta, and OpenAI — the same hyperscaler-capex theme now pulling Qualcomm deeper into the data-center chip race alongside Nvidia and Broadcom.

## What It Means

The through-line today is hyperscalers locking up custom silicon supply directly with chipmakers rather than relying solely on Nvidia — Qualcomm's $60B AWS deal is the clearest evidence yet that the AI-accelerator market is fragmenting into a multi-vendor race (Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD, now Qualcomm), which is bullish for chip-diversification plays and a signal to watch QCOM, AVGO, and AMD as AWS/Google/Meta keep shopping around for cheaper inference silicon. On the IPO side, Oura's filing and Anthropic's slipping-but-still-massive listing plans show investor appetite for growth stories remains strong even as broader indices wobble on trade-war and oil-price jitters — the Dow/S&P weakness alongside Nasdaq resilience today is itself a signal that money is rotating toward AI/semis as a relative safe haven. Near-term, keep an eye on QCOM follow-through, any Oura pricing-range update, and further Anthropic IPO-timeline headlines as the next catalysts.
