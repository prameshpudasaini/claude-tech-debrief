# 2026-08-10

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals (last 24 hours)

- **AMD acquires silicon-photonics startup Enosemi.** The deal brings Enosemi's custom optical-materials technology in-house to accelerate AMD's co-packaged optics roadmap for AI accelerators, part of the industry's push to solve data-center interconnect bottlenecks as GPU clusters scale.
- **Nscale closes its $1.65B acquisition of Anyscale.** The AI-cloud infrastructure provider is folding in Anyscale's Ray-based ML workload software to move up the stack from raw GPU capacity toward a full-stack AI hyperscaler — a bet that compute providers need software differentiation, not just chips, to compete with AWS, Azure and Google Cloud.
- **GlobalFoundries expands its chip partnership with Apple.** The deal broadens their existing supply agreement into wireless connectivity and power-management silicon, reinforcing US-based chip manufacturing capacity as Apple diversifies away from sole reliance on TSMC for select components.
- **Intel-TSMC US foundry joint-venture talks advance.** Reports indicate the two are nearing a preliminary agreement for TSMC to help operate Intel's domestic fabs (with a stake as high as 20%), with AMD, Broadcom and Nvidia reportedly approached as potential customers — a deal that would reshape US chip manufacturing and Intel's foundry turnaround story if finalized.
- **Micron raises its US investment pledge to $250B+ through 2035.** The memory maker is leaning further into domestic capacity as AI-driven demand for HBM and other advanced memory keeps outstripping supply, a tailwind for MU shares tied to the broader AI memory shortage narrative.
- **Palantir's blockbuster Q2 print keeps fueling the stock.** Revenue jumped 93% to $1.94B with record $3.37B in new contract value and a raised full-year guide to ~$8.15B, and shares have continued climbing since the report — a reminder of how sharply AI-software winners are being re-rated this earnings season.

## What it means

The through-line remains AI infrastructure consolidation: chipmakers are buying niche technology (AMD/Enosemi) to remove interconnect and packaging bottlenecks, cloud providers are acquiring software to differentiate raw compute (Nscale/Anyscale), and legacy chip manufacturers are locking in capacity and diversifying supply chains (GlobalFoundries/Apple, Micron's expansion, the Intel-TSMC talks). Watch AMD, Micron and Intel for reaction to the manufacturing realignment, and keep an eye on Palantir as a bellwether for how the market is pricing AI-software growth. With CoreWeave, Super Micro, Cisco and Applied Materials all reporting this week, expect more confirmation — or a reality check — on whether AI capex spending is translating into durable earnings growth across the stack.
