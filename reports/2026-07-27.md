# July 27, 2026 — Tech & Markets Morning Debrief

- **Samsung Electronics and Broadcom signed a memorandum of understanding worth more than $200B through 2030**, covering advanced memory supply, foundry cooperation, and sub-2nm production of Broadcom's next-gen ASICs — one of the largest chip-supply pacts of the year and a direct shot at TSMC's foundry dominance. AVGO shares wobbled before recovering toward $391 on the news.
- **SK Group agreed to supply roughly $750B in advanced memory chips to Nvidia and other global tech firms over five years**, part of a combined $950B wave of semiconductor partnerships unveiled at the Seoul AI summit — underscoring how severe the AI-driven memory (HBM) shortage has become.
- **Samsung SDS and Anthropic signed a strategic partnership** to identify new enterprise AI markets and jointly train AI engineers, deepening Anthropic's push into Asian enterprise accounts alongside its existing multibillion-dollar Azure/AWS compute commitments.
- **Tempus AI's $1.5B acquisition of genomics data firm Personalis** continues to close out, consolidating AI-driven precision-medicine data assets — a reminder that AI-healthcare M&A is accelerating alongside pure infrastructure deals.
- **General Fusion completed its Nasdaq debut under ticker GFUZ**, the first fusion-energy company to go public on a major U.S. exchange, opening a new public-market lane for next-gen clean-energy-tech bets.
- **Wall Street analysts piled on bullish AMD price-target hikes** (Wedbush to $600, Jefferies to $640, Melius to $660, Roth to $650) following its Q1 beat ($10.25B revenue, +38% YoY) — a bullish semiconductor signal heading into this week's hyperscaler earnings.
- **Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon all report earnings this week (July 27–30)**, the next real test of whether the ~$725B in 2026 AI capex guidance is converting into revenue, after Alphabet's capex-driven selloff last week set a cautious tone.
- **Plaid and Verkada are both in preliminary IPO talks with banks**, adding fintech and physical-security-tech names to a swelling IPO pipeline that already includes reported confidential filings from Anthropic (~$965B valuation) and OpenAI (~$852B+ valuation).

Today's dominant story is the AI supply chain, not AI software: Samsung, Broadcom, SK Group, and Nvidia just locked in nearly $1 trillion combined in multi-year memory and foundry commitments, confirming the HBM/memory shortage is now the binding constraint on AI buildout — watch **AVGO, Micron, and SK Hynix-linked names** for follow-through, and Samsung despite its own recent share weakness. Layered on top, bullish AMD analyst calls and a fresh wave of AI/fintech IPO chatter (Anthropic, OpenAI, Plaid, Verkada) signal investors are still willing to pay up for AI exposure even as they turn skeptical of hyperscaler capex discipline. The real swing factor for the next few days is earnings: Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon report July 27–30, and any capex guidance hike without matching revenue growth could extend the chip-sector pullback that started with Alphabet last week.
