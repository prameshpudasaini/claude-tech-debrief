# 2026-08-17

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Stripe finalizes $7B+ acquisition of OpenRouter.** Stripe closed its deal to buy the AI model-routing/gateway startup at a price topping $7 billion — more than 5x OpenRouter's ~$1.3B Series B valuation from just three months ago. The move turns Stripe into an AI-traffic billing and routing layer, not just a payments processor, and signals payments companies are racing to own infrastructure sitting on top of AI usage.
- **Nvidia scales back its OpenAI Ohio data center backstop to ~$105B (from a floated $250B).** Nvidia has agreed to fund up to $105 billion to support the Pike County, Ohio campus OpenAI will lease (up to 8 gigawatts of capacity, first 800MW online by 2028), after investors flagged concerns about Nvidia's financing risk exposure. Nvidia insists it isn't circular financing since "OpenAI will pay the lease" — but the size still makes NVDA a stock to watch on any follow-up financing news.
- **TTM Technologies to acquire Epiq Solutions for ~$1.1B in cash.** The PCB/electronics maker is buying the defense-focused software-defined-radio and RF systems provider to push further into missile defense, space, electronic warfare and ISR markets. TTMI shares rose premarket on the announcement as the deal shifts TTM's mix toward higher-margin, long-cycle defense revenue.
- **Anthropic reportedly in talks to buy Decart AI for ~$6B.** Sources say Anthropic is negotiating its largest-ever acquisition — an Israeli startup specializing in real-time generative video and GPU-efficiency software — at roughly a 50% premium to Decart's last private valuation (~$4B). The deal isn't finalized, but it would be a major signal that AI labs are now buying efficiency/infrastructure tech, not just talent.
- **AMD's Taalas acquisition moves toward Q4 close.** AMD's deal (announced Aug 6) to buy Toronto-based AI inference-chip startup Taalas — which "hardwires" AI models directly into silicon — remains on track, adding specialized inference hardware to AMD's Instinct/Helios/ROCm stack as it competes harder with Nvidia in inference workloads.
- **Naver, Nvidia and Brookfield's $10B Korea AI data center buildout continues to ripple.** Brookfield's up-to-$9B financing commitment plus Nvidia's $1B strategic investment (expanding Naver's Sejong facility from 55MW to 200MW by 2028) remains one of the largest sovereign-AI infrastructure bets in Asia and continues to draw investor attention to Nvidia's expanding off-balance-sheet financing footprint.
- **TSMC and Unitree Robotics remain in focus** as China's Unitree — the world's largest humanoid robot maker by sales — prepares to debut on Shanghai's STAR Market on Aug 19, a listing that was reportedly oversubscribed more than 8,000x by retail investors, underscoring how hot AI-robotics IPO demand has become in Asia.

## What it means

The dominant theme today is infrastructure and efficiency, not model-layer land grabs: Stripe buying billing/routing rails for AI traffic, Anthropic reportedly chasing GPU-efficiency tech, and AMD folding inference-optimized silicon into its stack all point to AI economics shifting from "build bigger models" to "make inference and usage cheaper to run and easier to bill." At the same time, financing structures around AI infrastructure are under real scrutiny — Nvidia trimming its OpenAI Ohio backstop from $250B to ~$105B after investor pushback on risk exposure is a signal that even the biggest AI-capex commitments are being pressure-tested. Watch NVDA and AMD on any follow-up financing or acquisition-close news, STRIPE-adjacent fintech/payments names on AI-infrastructure crossover plays, and TTMI as its defense-tech pivot plays out; Unitree's Aug 19 Shanghai listing is also worth watching as a read on global appetite for AI-robotics IPOs.
