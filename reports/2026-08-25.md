# 2026-08-25

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals

- **Nvidia is paying AI startup Poolside a $6B license fee** for its "Model Factory" software and hiring 109+ of its engineers to work on Nvidia's Nemotron open-weight models, while separately investing $1B in Poolside's remaining business at a $12–13B valuation. It's the same non-exclusive "license-and-hire" playbook Nvidia used with Groq late last year — a way to absorb AI talent and IP without a formal acquisition. Watch NVDA.
- **Infineon agreed to acquire Bangalore-based C2i Semiconductors** to strengthen its power-management chip lineup for AI data centers; C2i specializes in software-defined multiphase controllers and smart power stages that regulate the increasingly volatile power draw of AI processors. Terms weren't disclosed; the deal is expected to close in Q3 2026. Watch IFNNY.
- **Marvell shares jumped as much as 13% after disclosing Google received a $12.2B stock warrant** tied to a custom-chip partnership that could see Marvell earn up to $120B from Google over time, developing AI inference accelerators, storage and networking controllers for Google's TPU ecosystem. Watch MRVL and GOOGL.
- **Groq raised $350M at a $3.5B valuation — roughly half its prior valuation** — in a round Nvidia participated in, months after Nvidia struck a similar $20B non-exclusive licensing-and-hiring deal for Groq's inference technology and poached its founder and president. The falling valuation is a signal that Nvidia's "acqui-hire-lite" deals can leave the licensed startup diminished even as the tech giant benefits.
- **Nvidia reports fiscal Q2 earnings Wednesday (Aug 26)**, with Wall Street modeling roughly $91B in revenue (+95% YoY) and $2.09 adjusted EPS, driven by ~$85B in data-center sales; analyst price targets cluster at $275–$325 versus the stock's recent ~$219. It's the single biggest near-term catalyst for chip and AI-infrastructure stocks broadly. Watch NVDA, and MRVL/AMD/AVGO by extension.
- **OpenAI's IPO remains in motion after its confidential S-1 filing**, with the company targeting a public debut as soon as Q4 2026 (though some reports suggest a slip to 2027) at a valuation around $852B, revenue growing roughly 4x faster at this stage than Alphabet or Meta did, per the company. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading the offering.

## What it means

Nvidia is running the same playbook twice in under a year — pay a huge licensing fee, hire the bulk of a startup's engineers, and take a minority equity stake, rather than a formal acquisition (Groq in December, Poolside now) — a workaround that lets it absorb frontier AI talent while keeping antitrust exposure low, though Groq's halved valuation shows the "independent" company left behind can end up worse for it. Meanwhile the custom-ASIC land grab keeps widening: Marvell's Google warrant and Infineon's C2i buy both show second- and third-tier chip suppliers (not just Nvidia/AMD) capturing real upside as hyperscalers diversify silicon and power infrastructure. All of this sets up Wednesday's Nvidia earnings as the week's dominant catalyst — a beat-and-raise likely lifts the whole AI-chip complex (MRVL, AMD, AVGO, IFNNY), while any guidance wobble on margins or Blackwell ramp could hit sentiment across the sector, with OpenAI's eventual IPO timeline as the next mega-catalyst investors are tracking behind it.
