# August 6, 2026 — Tech & Markets Morning Debrief

- **AMD posted record Q2 revenue of $11.5 billion (EPS $1.66), beating estimates on both lines**, with data center sales more than doubling year-over-year to $6.7 billion (58% of total revenue) on strong EPYC and Instinct GPU demand. Shares still fell more than 8% as investors focused on rich valuations relative to Q3 guidance of $12.7–$13.3 billion.
- **SpaceX absorbed a massive 911.5 million-share lock-up unlock (~$109 billion worth, 1.45x the original IPO float) and still rose over 3%**, rebounding from the 13% slide that followed its first-ever public earnings report (Q2 revenue $7.8 billion, up 92% y/y, led by Starlink's $4.29 billion).
- **Samsung Electronics signed a memorandum of understanding with Broadcom projected to exceed $200 billion through 2030**, expanding cooperation on memory chips, contract manufacturing, and advanced packaging — a direct challenge to TSMC's foundry dominance.
- **SK Group and Nvidia signed letters of intent for a partnership Nvidia estimates could be worth $500 billion-plus over several years**, covering a 2-gigawatt AI data center in Korea built on Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform and a long-term SK hynix HBM4 memory supply agreement.
- **AMD kept extending its hyperscaler footprint**: Microsoft Azure said it will deploy AMD's Helios rack-scale systems (Instinct MI455 GPUs) for AI inference, while Meta separately expanded its AMD GPU commitment to up to six gigawatts worth of chips — both wins chip away at Nvidia's near-monopoly on AI compute.
- **Nvidia announced an $80 billion stock buyback alongside a dividend increase**, a capital-return signal even as the company continues to flag softness in China data-center revenue.
- **SanDisk and Western Digital shares retreated despite the broader AI-driven memory boom**, as both issued more subdued forward guidance than the market wanted — a reminder that even "picks-and-shovels" AI suppliers aren't immune to valuation scrutiny.

AI infrastructure spending is still the market's dominant storyline, but the shape of it is shifting: instead of one more hyperscaler-Nvidia GPU order, the big news is the chip *supply chain* itself consolidating and diversifying — Samsung-Broadcom and SK-Nvidia are multi-year, hundred-billion-dollar bets on memory and foundry capacity, while AMD is proving out as a legitimate second source with real Microsoft and Meta commitments. At the same time, investors are getting pickier: AMD, SpaceX, SanDisk, and Western Digital all delivered strong AI-linked numbers this week and still sold off or wobbled, suggesting the bar for "good enough" is rising fast. Watch **AMD** and **NVDA** for continued swings on capex/guidance commentary, **AVGO** and Korea's chip suppliers on the new mega-partnerships, and **SNDK**/**WDC** as a gauge of whether the memory-pricing tailwind is starting to lose steam.
