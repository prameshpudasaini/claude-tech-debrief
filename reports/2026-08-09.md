# 2026-08-09

## Tech & Market-Moving Deals (last 24 hours)

- **AMD acquires AI inference chip startup Taalas.** The Toronto-based startup, which hardwires trained AI model weights directly into silicon to cut memory overhead, becomes AMD's second Canadian AI-chip acquisition in about a year; terms weren't disclosed (Taalas had raised $219M), and the deal is expected to close in Q4 2026 as AMD sharpens its challenge to Nvidia in inference workloads.
- **SpaceX and Tesla commit $16.8B to build "Terafab," a Texas AI chip superfactory.** The Grimes County, Texas facility will combine logic, memory, packaging and testing on a 100M+ square-foot site targeting 1 terawatt of annual compute hardware output for Optimus robots, Cybercabs and space-based data centers — with the multi-phase buildout potentially exceeding $100B in total investment.
- **Data-center operator Switch confidentially files for a US IPO.** Bloomberg reports the Las Vegas-based AI infrastructure company is targeting a listing as soon as November at a valuation approaching $80 billion, working with Bank of America, Citigroup, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley to raise up to $10 billion — a16z co-founder Ben Horowitz is also joining its board.
- **AMD signs strategic AI partnership with South Korea's government.** The deal, aimed at building out Korea's sovereign AI ecosystem, has AMD contributing compute technology, open software and technical expertise — another sign chipmakers are racing to lock up national-scale AI infrastructure commitments beyond Big Tech.
- **Robinhood advances its Robinhood Ventures Fund II, structured to list via IPO.** The brokerage is raising a new venture fund giving retail investors direct share allocations (up to 400,000 shares) into pre-IPO tech names, extending its push to democratize access to private AI and startup equity.

## What it means

The pattern continues: capital is pouring into the physical infrastructure of AI — chip fabs (Tesla/SpaceX's Terafab), data centers (Switch's IPO filing) and specialized inference silicon (AMD-Taalas) — rather than into software applications. Watch AMD and TSLA for continued reaction to their infrastructure bets, and expect the Switch filing to add momentum to the AI-infra IPO pipeline that SpaceX and others have opened up this year. Collectively, these moves signal that the AI buildout is entering a capital-intensive, build-your-own-supply-chain phase, with chipmakers and hyperscalers increasingly bypassing traditional suppliers (Nvidia, TSMC) to secure compute directly — a trend worth tracking for its implications on margins across the semiconductor sector.
